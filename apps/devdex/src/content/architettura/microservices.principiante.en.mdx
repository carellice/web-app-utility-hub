# Microservices — The basics

A **microservice** system is a collection of services:

- **Small** and focused on a business capability.
- **Autonomous**: each has its own code, its own DB, its own deploy.
- They communicate over the network: HTTP/REST, gRPC, message queues.

```text
[ Web ] → [ Auth ] → [ Orders ] → [ Payments ]
                           ↘  [ Notifications ]
```

## Why do it?

- **Independent scalability**: the "search" service scales differently from
  "checkout".
- **Team independence**: a team owns a service end-to-end (Conway's law).
- **Heterogeneous tech**: use the right tool per problem.
- **Frequent deploys**: releasing one does not block the others.

## Why NOT (right away)

- Operational complexity: monitoring, tracing, deploy, networking.
- Distributed transactions (sagas, eventual consistency).
- Latency: every call is over the network.
- Infrastructure costs.

Sam Newman's rule: **"Don't start with microservices"**. Start with a modular
monolith, and split only when you have concrete pain.

## Communication

- **Synchronous** (REST/gRPC): simple, but couples availability.
- **Asynchronous** (Kafka, RabbitMQ, SQS): more resilient, but more complex
  (idempotency, ordering).

## Microservices ≠ "many small services"

If 5 services always deploy together and share a DB → you have a **distributed
monolith**: worse than a monolith, because you pay network costs without the
independence benefits.
